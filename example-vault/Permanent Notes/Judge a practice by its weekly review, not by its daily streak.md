## Judge a practice by its weekly review, not by its daily streak

The day is the wrong unit for judging a practice. A daily streak makes one missed day look like failure and invites quitting; a weekly total absorbs the miss and still shows the trend. The week is also the smallest window in which you can tell whether the practice is paying off at all.

**Operating rule**: Replace any consecutive-day counter with a weekly count, and review it at the same point every week.

**Test**: Would one missed day today change how I feel about the whole practice? If yes, I am measuring by the streak.

**Boundary conditions**: Practices that only work daily (medication) need a daily check, not a weekly one.

**Why it matters**: Streak counters are the default in most tracking tools, and they punish the exact moment when support is most needed.

**Evidence**: Ellwood's comparison of streak counters versus weekly totals, and Hartigan's weekly rhythm data on estimate accuracy.

**Your extension**: Two missed days ended a 40-day running streak and three weeks of running with it; the weekly count has not let that happen since.

**Connections**:
- Contrasts with [[A new routine sticks when it is small enough to run on a bad day]] in that one protects the streak by size, the other removes the streak
- Supports [[Team rituals need an owner and a cue, not just a calendar slot]] because the weekly review is itself a ritual that needs an owner
