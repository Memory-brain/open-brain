## A new routine sticks when it is small enough to run on a bad day

A routine is decided on its worst day, not its best. If the smallest acceptable version does not fit a sick Tuesday, it will be skipped, and a skipped routine is the first step to a dropped one. This holds for a personal habit and for a team ritual alike: three written lines survive a busy week where a 30-minute meeting does not.

**Operating rule**: Cut every new routine down until it fits the worst day, then let it grow only after it has survived a month.

**Test**: Can I do the smallest version in under five minutes while tired? If not, it is too big.

**Boundary conditions**: Does not apply to routines whose value comes from depth in one sitting (a weekly long run for a marathon); there the size is the point.

**Why it matters**: Most people size a new routine for motivation on day one; the routine then fails on day nine.

**Evidence**: Two independent sources: the drop-out comparison in The Small Loop (tiny versions still running at eight weeks) and the three-line Friday update in Teams of Five.

**Your extension**: My reading habit and our team update both survived only after I cut them to a size that felt almost silly.
