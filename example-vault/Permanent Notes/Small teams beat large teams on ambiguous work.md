## Small teams beat large teams on ambiguous work

When the work is ambiguous, most of the effort goes into agreeing what the work is. A small team does that agreeing in passing; a large one needs meetings to do it, and the meetings grow faster than the headcount.

**Operating rule**: Staff exploratory work with the smallest team that has the needed skills; add people only once the shape of the work is settled.

**Test**: Can each member describe what every other member is doing this week? If not, the team is already too big for ambiguous work.

**Boundary conditions**: Work that is large but well understood (a migration with a known plan) can absorb more people.

**Why it matters**: The default response to a slow project is to add people, which slows ambiguous work further.

**Evidence**: Hartigan's meeting counts by team size.
