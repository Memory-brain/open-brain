## Team rituals need an owner and a cue, not just a calendar slot

A calendar slot is a clock-time trigger, and clock-time triggers are the weakest kind. A team ritual lasts when it has two things a slot does not give it: one person whose job is to restart it after a skip, and an anchor in something the team already does (the release, the Monday plan).

**Operating rule**: For every recurring ritual, write down its owner and the existing event it follows; drop any ritual that has neither.

**Test**: If this ritual is skipped once, who restarts it, and what reminds them? No answer means it is already dying.

**Boundary conditions**: Rituals imposed from outside the team (a compliance review) survive without an owner because someone else enforces them.

**Why it matters**: Teams treat a calendar invite as if it were a commitment; it is only a reminder.

**Evidence**: Hartigan's two-year count of rituals (all five survivors had an owner) and Ellwood's cue-versus-clock comparison, from two independent fields.

**Your extension**: Our retrospective died after one holiday skip; it came back only when one person owned it and it followed the release.
