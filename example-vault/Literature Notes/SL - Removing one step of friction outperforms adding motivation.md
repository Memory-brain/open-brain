## Removing one step of friction outperforms adding motivation

Ellwood treats friction as the main variable people ignore. Before looking for more motivation, she asks how many steps sit between the trigger and the behavior, and removes one. Laying out running shoes the night before is her running example.

> "Every step between the cue and the act is a place to quit." — Mara Ellwood, Ellwood, 2019, ch. 5

**Evidence**: In one of her small experiments, moving the guitar from its case to a stand in the living room tripled weekly practice sessions for the participants who tried it.

**Source pointer**: Ellwood, 2019, ch. 5.
