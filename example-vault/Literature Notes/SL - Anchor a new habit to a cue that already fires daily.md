## Anchor a new habit to a cue that already fires daily

Ellwood recommends choosing the trigger before choosing the behavior. The best trigger is an event that already happens reliably (finishing coffee, closing the laptop) rather than a time on the clock, because the event carries its own context and the clock does not.

> "A clock tells you when; a cue tells you where you are." — Mara Ellwood, Ellwood, 2019, ch. 3

**Evidence**: Volunteers who anchored the new behavior to an existing event logged about twice as many repetitions in the first month as those who set a reminder for a fixed time.

**Source pointer**: Ellwood, 2019, ch. 3.

## Your extension

I tried a 7am reminder for stretching for a month and skipped it a third of the time. Moving it to 'right after I put the kettle on' fixed it within a week.
